Research

Learning from a changing sky.

I build physical simulators and inference methods to connect time-domain observations to the systems that produce them.

A real gravitational lens

One quasar.
Four views of a changing disk.

In RX J1131−1231, a foreground galaxy bends the light of a distant quasar into four images. Each follows a different path, so the same intrinsic variations arrive at different times.

Stars in the lens galaxy add a different microlensing signal to each image. Separating these signals lets us study accretion disks and compact objects while measuring the time delays that probe cosmic expansion.

Rubin Observatory / LSST

A decade of light.
New ways to read it.

The Legacy Survey of Space and Time repeatedly images the southern sky in six bands. For quasars, that means long, multicolor records of accretion and microlensing, with irregular sampling and seasonal gaps.

I am developing latent-SDE models trained on physical simulations to make use of these light curves as they grow. My goal is to connect survey monitoring to measurements of disk structure, microlens populations, and cosmological time delays, while guiding follow-up of the most informative events.

Quasar accretion disks

Reverberation measures how an accretion disk responds to changing illumination. Microlensing magnifies different parts of the disk. Together, they can reveal its size, temperature structure, and inner-disk structure.

Dynamical microlensing

Stars move, so the magnification pattern evolves. I developed Microcaustics to make these simulations practical on GPUs and study how stellar motion and mass distributions affect inference.

Cosmology and compact objects

Strongly lensed quasars probe compact matter in distant galaxies and provide time delays for measuring cosmic expansion. My goal is joint time-series inference that accounts for microlensing rather than treating it only as a nuisance.

Machine learning for inference

From sparse observations
to physical models.

My latent stochastic differential equation (SDE) framework reconstructs irregular quasar light curves and infers black-hole and accretion-disk properties. It combines learned variability with a physical model of disk reprocessing.

Current work extends this approach to simulation-based inference with a pretrained Transformer encoder and tests it on observed ZTF and DES quasars. For lensed systems, I am developing methods to identify past crossings and forecast future high-magnification events for follow-up.

Preprints and submitted papers

  1. 2026

    Submitted to MNRAS

    Microcaustics: Fast GPU-Accelerated Dynamical Microlensing of Time-Dependent Extended Sources

    J. Fagin, C. Stone, J. H.-H. Chan, S. Miskiewicz, H. Best, P. Sharma, M. O’Dowd, L. Perreault-Levasseur, Y. Hezaveh, and S. Birrer

    An open-source GPU simulator for dynamical microlensing of quasars and other time-dependent extended sources. Across our benchmarks, it generates ten-year multiband light curves in an amortized median of 0.68 seconds on a consumer GPU, with a median error of 0.35 millimagnitudes and approximately 780 times the throughput of the GPU implementation used for comparison. This makes large-scale dynamical microlensing simulations practical for Bayesian inference and machine-learning training sets.

    arXiv ↗Code ↗
  2. 2026

    Submitted to ApJ

    Modeling Multi-band Active Galactic Nuclei Lightcurves Using an Self-supervised Attentive Neural Process Network

    T. Shen, C. Xin, J. Fagin, A. Villar, M. J. Graham, and K. E. S. Ford

    We develop an attentive neural-process model to reconstruct multiband quasar variability and infer black hole mass, Eddington ratio, and variability timescale. Its reconstruction component learns directly from sparse, noisy observations without requiring the underlying noiseless light curves, making it suitable for fine-tuning on real survey data. Tests with simulated LSST observations demonstrate reconstruction accuracy comparable to a Gaussian-process baseline and recovery of the underlying variability statistics.

    arXiv ↗

  3. Submitted to A&A

    Automated Caustic-Crossing Detection and Accretion-Disk Inference from Quasar Microlensing

    J. H.-H. Chan, J. Fagin, M. O’Dowd, and S. Miskiewicz

    We train a transformer neural network on dynamical microlensing simulations to identify caustic crossings and infer black hole mass and accretion-disk structure. Applied to 14 years of five-band observations of Q2237+0305, it provides independent numerical confirmation of a previously proposed double caustic crossing and identifies additional candidates. The inferred disk temperature profile is shallower than the standard thin-disk prediction.

Work in preparation

  1. In preparation

    Neglecting Dynamical Microlensing Can Bias Accretion Disk Size Measurements at a Population Level

    J. Fagin, J. H.-H. Chan, C. Stone, H. Best, P. Sharma, S. Miskiewicz, M. O’Dowd, L. Perreault-Levasseur, Y. Hezaveh, and S. Birrer

    We use Microcaustics to isolate the effects of independent microlens motions and a distribution of microlens masses on quasar microlensing. In our simulated population analyses, models trained without dynamical microlensing infer disk radii that are 6–28% smaller, while dynamical microlensing produces more frequent caustic crossings and shorter, more frequent high-magnification events. Omitting the microlens mass distribution produces smaller, configuration-dependent disk-size biases.

  2. In preparation

    Physics-Embedded Simulation-Based Inference of Quasar Accretion Disk Reprocessing with Latent Stochastic Differential Equations

    J. Fagin, J. H.-H. Chan, H. Best, M. O’Dowd, K. E. S. Ford, M. J. Graham, and V. A. Villar

    We combine latent stochastic differential equations with a physical accretion-disk model to jointly reconstruct quasar variability and infer black hole masses, disk properties, and continuum reverberation delays. Trained using more than 100 million simulated light curves, the model achieves a black hole mass RMSE of 0.24 dex on standard LSST simulations. We also test it on 550 ZTF and 182 DES quasars, finding roughly 0.45 dex differences from spectroscopic virial mass estimates while identifying remaining systematic offsets.

  3. In preparation

    GOLI-net: Deep-learning deblending of strongly lensed quasar–galaxy fiber spectra

    M. Espinoza Ortiz, T. Anguita, and J. Fagin

    A deep-learning model for separating quasar and lens-galaxy light in blended 4MOST fiber spectra. We test it on real blended 4MOST observations to recover the individual spectral components of strongly lensed quasar systems.

Selected refereed publications

  1. 2025

    The Astrophysical Journal · 988, 59

    Joint Modeling of Quasar Variability and Accretion Disk Reprocessing using Latent Stochastic Differential Equations

    J. Fagin, J. H.-H. Chan, H. Best, M. O’Dowd, K. E. S. Ford, M. J. Graham, J. W. Park, and V. A. Villar

    We embed a differentiable accretion-disk simulator within a latent-SDE neural network, connecting the reconstructed driving variability directly to the disk’s wavelength-dependent response. On simulated LSST observations, the model jointly infers disk properties and interband delays, achieving a black hole mass RMSE of 0.33 dex and outperforming the Gaussian-process reconstruction baseline. Tests with different driving signals show that it can recover disk properties even when the variability differs from its training assumptions.

  2. 2025

    The Astrophysical Journal · 981, 61

    Predicting High-magnification Events in Microlensed Quasars in the Era of LSST using Recurrent Neural Networks

    J. Fagin, E. Paic, F. Neira, H. Best, T. Anguita, M. Millon, M. O’Dowd, D. Sluse, and G. Vernardos

    We develop a recurrent neural network to forecast microlensing high-magnification events from sparse, noisy multiband observations. With simulated LSST monitoring, it predicts approximately 55% of event peaks, and tests of alternative observing strategies demonstrate the importance of multiband coverage and seasonal gaps. These forecasts could trigger follow-up observations that probe the inner regions of quasar accretion disks.

  3. 2025

    Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society · 539, 1269

    Amoeba: An AGN Model of Optical Emissions Beyond steady-state Accretion discs

    H. Best, M. O’Dowd, J. Fagin, J. H.-H. Chan, and B. Ierace

    An open-source framework that combines accretion disks, broad-line regions, dusty obscuration, and intrinsic variability in a spatially resolved AGN model. It simulates how reverberation and microlensing jointly shape multiband light curves, including contamination from emission lines and changes in observed response delays. The framework supports both interpretation of observations and generation of realistic training data.

  4. 2025

    The Astrophysical Journal · 978, 54

    Reverberation Mapping of Lamp-post and Wind Structures in Accretion Thin Disks

    J. H.-H. Chan, J. Fagin, H. Best, and M. J. O’Dowd

    We investigate how disk winds and departures from blackbody emission affect continuum reverberation measurements and the apparent sizes of accretion disks. Fits to four well-monitored AGNs favour a shallower temperature profile, approximately T ∝ R−0.66, and a color-correction factor near 1.6. We also derive efficient analytical expressions for interpreting wavelength-dependent lags and simulating quasar variability.

  5. 2024

    The Astrophysical Journal · 965, 104

    Latent Stochastic Differential Equations for Modeling Quasar Variability and Inferring Black Hole Properties

    J. Fagin, J. W. Park, H. Best, J. H.-H. Chan, K. E. S. Ford, M. J. Graham, V. A. Villar, S. Ho, and M. O’Dowd

    We introduce latent stochastic differential equations for jointly reconstructing multiband quasar light curves and inferring black hole and disk properties. On simulated LSST observations, the model reconstructs variability across irregular sampling and seasonal gaps, with modestly better accuracy than a multioutput Gaussian-process baseline. It also estimates parameter uncertainties, with the strongest constraints on variability properties and useful information about black hole mass and disk structure.

  6. 2024

    Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society · 532, 2248

    Measuring the Substructure Mass Power Spectrum of 23 SLACS Strong Galaxy-Galaxy Lenses with Convolutional Neural Networks

    J. Fagin, G. Vernardos, G. Tsagkatakis, Y. Pantazis, A. J. Shajib, and M. O’Dowd

    We use convolutional neural networks to constrain the statistical properties of small-scale mass perturbations in Hubble Space Telescope images of 23 strong gravitational lenses. Three training strategies give broadly consistent results, suggesting substructure with substantial power on small spatial scales within the adopted model. This approach probes the collective imprint of substructure, helping develop tests of the small-scale structure that distinguishes cold and warm dark matter models.

    Paper ↗arXiv ↗Code ↗
  7. 2024

    Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society · 531, 1095

    Resolving the Vicinity of Supermassive Black Holes with Gravitational Microlensing

    H. Best, J. Fagin, G. Vernardos, and M. O’Dowd

    We show how microlensing caustic crossings reveal the inner structure of relativistic accretion disks, including a characteristic feature associated with the innermost stable circular orbit. On simulated crossings, convolutional neural networks recover black hole mass with a scatter of about 0.19 dex and disk inclination to about 7°, while also constraining the disk’s orientation relative to the caustic. We apply the approach to observed caustic-crossing events in Q2237+0305.
